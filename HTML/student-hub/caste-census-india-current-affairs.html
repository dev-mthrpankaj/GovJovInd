Caste Census in India: Why It's Controversial and What You Should Know

Few current affairs topics combine history, sociology, and politics as neatly as the caste census debate. It shows up repeatedly in newspapers, editorial pages, and GK sections of competitive exams. This explainer breaks down the background, the latest state-level developments, and the arguments from both sides in plain language.

1. A Quick History of Caste Counting in India

India last collected comprehensive caste-wise data for the entire population in the 1931 Census, under British administration. After Independence, the government decided to stop enumerating all castes, continuing to record only Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST) figures through the regular Census, in order to avoid what officials at the time called deepening social divisions.

This means that reservation policy for Other Backward Classes (OBCs), first shaped nationally by the Mandal Commission report, has largely relied on estimates rather than a fresh, complete count. That data gap is the root of today's demand for a proper caste census.

2. What Has Happened Recently

Bihar carried out its own state-level caste survey and released the findings, which had a notable political impact and encouraged other states and opposition parties to push harder for a nationwide exercise. Karnataka commissioned a Socio-Economic and Educational Survey of its own, but its release has been contested, with some dominant communities objecting to how their population share is represented and demanding a fresh survey using improved methodology.

At the national level, there has been continuing debate over whether the upcoming decennial Census should include a full caste enumeration column, moving beyond the SC/ST-only approach that has been followed for decades.

3. The Arguments For and Against

In favour: Supporters argue that accurate, up-to-date caste data is necessary for evidence-based welfare planning and for deciding which communities genuinely need affirmative action support. They say caste discrimination already exists in daily life, so measuring it honestly cannot make things worse, only better informed.

Against, or cautious: Critics worry that a formal caste count could harden group identities, encourage competitive claims for quota status among different communities, and fuel demands to raise or scrap the Supreme Court-mandated cap on total reservations. Some also flag the sheer administrative complexity of accurately classifying thousands of caste and sub-caste groups across the country.

4. Static GK Connect for Exams

  • 1931 Census: Last full caste enumeration before Independence.
  • 1951 onwards: Only SC/ST data collected in the Census; OBC estimates from separate surveys and commissions.
  • Mandal Commission (1980 report): Recommended 27% reservation for OBCs in central government jobs and education.
  • Indra Sawhney case (1992): Supreme Court upheld OBC reservation but capped total reservations at 50%, with limited exceptions.
  • Census Act, 1948: The legal basis for conducting the decennial Census in India.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

When was the last full caste census conducted in India?

The last time complete caste-wise data was collected for all communities was in the 1931 Census, conducted under British rule. After Independence, India continued collecting data only for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, not for other caste groups.

Why is a caste census considered controversial?

Supporters say accurate caste data is essential for designing fair welfare and reservation policies, since current quotas are based on decades-old estimates. Critics worry it could harden caste identities and fuel demands to raise or remove the existing cap on reservation quotas, deepening social and political divisions.

Which states have already conducted their own caste surveys?

Bihar conducted and released a state-level caste survey that had significant political impact. Karnataka commissioned a Socio-Economic and Educational Survey that has faced pushback from some dominant communities. Candidates should check official state government sources for the latest status of any survey.

Note for candidates: Specific population percentages, survey release dates, and quota-related court rulings are updated frequently. Always verify the latest figures from the Census of India's official website or respective state government portals before using them in an exam or interview answer.
