SSC CHSL Syllabus 2026: New Sectional Timer, Tier 1 & Tier 2 Exam Pattern

SSC CHSL Syllabus 2026 now comes with a major exam-pattern change: SSC has introduced a 15-minute sectional timer for each Tier 1 subject. This guide explains the official Tier 1 and Tier 2 timings, complete syllabus, negative marking, computer test, skill/typing rules, qualifying standards and final-merit logic from the 2026 notification.

Key Takeaways

  • New in Tier 1: SSC CHSL 2026 has a fixed 15-minute timer for each subject; the total remains 60 minutes.
  • Tier 1 pattern: 25 questions and 50 marks each from English, General Intelligence, Quantitative Aptitude and General Awareness, with 0.50 mark deducted for every wrong answer.
  • Tier 2 also uses subject timers: Maths 30 min, Reasoning 30 min, English 40 min, GA 20 min, followed by a 15-minute Computer Knowledge Test.
  • Final merit: aggregate marks of Tier 2 Section I + Section II decide merit, subject to qualifying Computer Knowledge and Skill/Typing Test.
  • Typing standard: LDC/JSA requires 35 wpm English or 30 wpm Hindi; DEO skill tests require 15,000 or 8,000 KDPH depending on the post.
  • Weightage tables are trends, not official allotments: use them only to prioritise revision, not to skip the remaining syllabus.

SSC CHSL Syllabus 2026 at a glance

Tier 1 is the shortlisting stage, but the 2026 paper is no longer a freely managed 60-minute test: each of the four subjects closes after its own 15-minute slot. Tier 2 is decisive for final merit and also has fixed subject-wise timers, followed by qualifying Computer Knowledge and Skill/Typing components.

Tier 1 Focus15-minute subject sprints: speed + accuracy without borrowing time
Tier 2 FocusFixed subject timers + 360 merit marks + qualifying computer/skill tests

1. SSC CHSL Exam Pattern 2026: New Sectional Timers in Tier 1 and Tier 2

The official SSC CHSL 2026 notification dated 7 September 2026 confirms a major change in the computer-based exam: Tier 1 now has a 15-minute sectional timer for each subject. The overall Tier 1 duration remains 60 minutes, but candidates can no longer use extra time saved in one subject for another subject.

Tier 2 is conducted in two sessions on the same day. Session I contains Section I, Section II and the Computer Knowledge Test; Session II contains the post-specific Skill Test or Typing Test. Final merit is based on the scored parts of Tier 2, not Tier 1.

What changed for preparation? Earlier-style mock practice where you freely shifted the full 60 minutes across Tier 1 subjects is no longer enough. Your mocks should now reproduce four separate 15-minute subject windows.

2. SSC CHSL Tier 1 Syllabus 2026 - Subject-Wise Topics

Tier 1 has four equal sections. That equal split is important: a strong candidate cannot afford to prepare only Maths and Reasoning while ignoring English or General Awareness.

General Awareness Syllabus

The General Awareness section tests static GK, science awareness and recent events. For preparation, it is sensible to covering current affairs from roughly the previous six to nine months alongside core static topics.

  • History; Culture; Geography; Economic Scene; General Policy and Indian Polity
  • India and neighbouring countries
  • General Science and scientific research
  • Books and Authors; Awards and Honours
  • Sports GK, Indian GK and current affairs

General Intelligence and Reasoning Syllabus

  • Semantic, symbolic and number analogy
  • Trends, figural analogy and space orientation
  • Semantic, symbolic and number classification
  • Venn diagrams, drawing inferences and figural classification
  • Number and figural series; embedded figures
  • Critical thinking, problem solving and emotional intelligence
  • Word building, coding-decoding and numerical operations
  • Punched-hole/pattern folding and unfolding; pattern completion

English Language Syllabus

  • Spot the Error and Fill in the Blanks
  • Synonyms/Homonyms and Antonyms
  • Spellings / detecting misspelt words
  • Idioms and Phrases; One Word Substitution
  • Improvement of Sentences
  • Active and Passive Voice
  • Direct and Indirect Narration
  • Sentence-part and passage rearrangement
  • Cloze Passage and Reading Comprehension

Quantitative Aptitude Syllabus

  • Number System: whole numbers, decimals, fractions and relationships between numbers
  • Fundamental Arithmetic: percentage, ratio and proportion, square roots, averages, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, discount, partnership, mixture and allegation, time and distance, time and work
  • Mensuration: triangles, quadrilaterals, polygons, circles, prisms, cones, cylinders, spheres, hemispheres, parallelepipeds and pyramids
  • Algebra: basic school algebra identities and graphs of linear equations
  • Geometry: triangles, congruence and similarity, circles, chords, tangents and common tangents
  • Trigonometry: ratios, identities, complementary angles, heights and distances
  • Statistics and Charts: histogram, frequency polygon, bar diagram and pie chart

3. Tier 1 Questions, Sectional Timer and Negative Marking

SSC has retained the 100-question, 200-mark Tier 1 structure, but the 2026 notification introduces a sectional timer. Each subject gets exactly 15 minutes. For candidates eligible for scribe/compensatory time under the specified SSC provisions, each subject gets 20 minutes, making the total 80 minutes.

Tier 1 carries a penalty of 0.50 mark for every wrong answer. Because unused time from one subject cannot rescue another subject, accuracy under a tight 15-minute window becomes more important than attempting every question.

Preparation implication: practise English, Reasoning, Maths and GA as four independent 15-minute tests. Track attempts, accuracy and time pressure separately for each subject.

4. SSC CHSL Tier 2 Syllabus 2026, Sectional Timers and Mains Pattern

Tier 2 is the decisive scoring stage for final merit. It is held in two sessions on the same day. Session I lasts 2 hours 15 minutes and contains Section I, Section II and Section III. After a break for re-registration, Session II conducts the Skill Test or Typing Test.

Official SSC CHSL Tier 2 2026 modules, marks and timers
SectionSubjectQuestionsMarksRegular Time
Section I - Part AMathematical Abilities309030 min
Section I - Part BReasoning & General Intelligence309030 min
Section II - Part AEnglish Language & Comprehension4012040 min
Section II - Part BGeneral Awareness206020 min
Section IIIComputer Knowledge Test154515 min
Section IV / Session IISkill Test / Typing TestQualifyingQualifying10 or 15 min, post-dependent

For eligible scribe candidates, SSC provides additional time: Mathematics and Reasoning get 40 minutes each; English gets 55 minutes; General Awareness gets 25 minutes; Computer Knowledge gets 20 minutes. The Skill/Typing duration also increases by five minutes where applicable.

Section I closes after its allotted hour and Section II starts immediately. Section II also closes after its allotted hour, after which the 15-minute Computer Knowledge Test begins. There is 1 mark negative marking for every wrong answer in Sections I, II and III.

All four Tier 2 sections are mandatory to qualify. Section III (Computer Knowledge) and Section IV (Skill/Typing) are qualifying in nature, while the marks of Section I + Section II form the merit score.

Mathematical Abilities - Tier 2

Official topics include number systems; percentages, ratio and proportion, square roots, averages, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, discount, partnership, mixture and alligation, time and distance, time and work; algebra; geometry; mensuration; trigonometry; and statistics and probability, including mean, median, mode, standard deviation and simple probability.

Reasoning and General Intelligence - Tier 2

Prepare verbal and non-verbal reasoning including semantic and figural analogy, symbolic operations, classification, series, space orientation, Venn diagrams, drawing inferences, coding-decoding, embedded figures, punched-hole/pattern folding, critical thinking, problem solving, emotional intelligence, word building and social intelligence.

English Language and Comprehension - Tier 2

The official syllabus covers vocabulary, grammar, sentence structure, synonyms/homonyms, antonyms, error spotting, fill in the blanks, spellings, idioms and phrases, one-word substitution, sentence improvement, active/passive voice, direct/indirect narration, sentence shuffling, cloze passage and comprehension. SSC also specifies two or more comprehension paragraphs, including a simple book/story passage and another based on a current-affairs editorial or report.

General Awareness - Tier 2

Questions cover current events and everyday scientific awareness, along with India and neighbouring countries, especially History, Culture, Geography, Economic Scene, General Policy and Scientific Research.

Computer Knowledge Test - Qualifying but Mandatory

  • Computer organisation, CPU, input/output devices, memory, memory organisation, backup devices and ports
  • Windows Explorer and keyboard shortcuts
  • Windows operating system and basics of MS Word, MS Excel and PowerPoint
  • Web browsing/searching, downloading/uploading, email account management and e-banking
  • Networking devices and protocols, cyber-security threats such as hacking, viruses, worms and Trojans, and preventive measures

5. SSC CHSL Skill Test and Typing Test Rules

Section IV is conducted in Session II on the same day as Tier 2 Session I and is qualifying in nature. DEO candidates cannot skip the Skill Test. For LDC/JSA and other non-DEO posts, the Typing Test is mandatory except for candidates who receive the specific medical exemption allowed by SSC.

Official SSC CHSL 2026 typing and data-entry standards
PostTestRequired StandardRegular DurationEligible Scribe Duration
DEO / DEO Grade A in specified Ministries/DepartmentsData Entry Skill Test15,000 key depressions per hour15 min20 min
DEO / DEO Grade A in other DepartmentsData Entry Skill Test8,000 key depressions per hour15 min20 min
LDC / JSATyping TestEnglish: 35 wpm (about 10,500 KDPH) or Hindi: 30 wpm (about 9,000 KDPH)10 min15 min

For the 15,000 KDPH DEO test, SSC specifies English matter of about 3,700-4,000 key depressions; for the 8,000 KDPH test, about 2,000-2,200 key depressions. LDC/JSA speed is judged from the accuracy of typing a given text passage on a computer.

LDC/JSA candidates choose either English or Hindi as the typing medium in the application form and that choice is final. Candidates choosing Hindi must type in Mangal Font; use of another font can lead to non-qualification.

Practical tip: practise above the bare minimum speed while protecting accuracy. Under timed testing, a small speed buffer is much safer than trying to sit exactly at 35 wpm or 30 wpm.

6. Minimum Qualifying Marks and Final Merit Logic

Official minimum qualifying marks for Tier 1 and Tier 2 Sections I-III
CategoryMinimum Qualifying Marks
UR30%
OBC / EWS25%
All other categories20%

These percentages apply as minimum qualifying standards in Tier 1 and in Sections I, II and III of Tier 2. They are only qualifying floors; actual cut-offs can be higher and SSC may set separate category-wise cut-offs for different post groups.

Tier 1 is used to shortlist candidates for Tier 2. In Tier 2, every candidate must appear in all four sections and qualify all of them. Computer Knowledge (Section III) and Skill/Typing (Section IV) are qualifying in nature.

Final merit formula: SSC states that the final merit list is prepared from the aggregate marks of Section I + Section II of Tier 2, subject to qualifying Sections III and IV. That means the merit-bearing objective total is 360 marks (180 + 180), not the full 405 including the qualifying Computer Knowledge marks.

Post/department preferences submitted through SSC's Online Option Form also matter at final allocation. If a candidate does not submit preferences within the stipulated window, the candidate is not considered for any post in the final result.

8. How to Prepare the SSC CHSL Syllabus Efficiently

  1. Map every topic once: make a checklist for English, Reasoning, Maths and GA before starting random mocks.
  2. Prioritise high-frequency chapters: arithmetic, analogy/classification/series, grammar, cloze, static GK and current affairs deserve repeated revision.
  3. Use exact 15-minute Tier 1 drills: practise each subject in its own fixed window because unused time cannot be shifted to another section.
  4. Mirror Tier 2 timers too: practise Maths 30 min, Reasoning 30 min, English 40 min, GA 20 min and Computer 15 min so exam-day timing feels normal.
  5. Analyse every mock: after each test, separate conceptual errors, calculation mistakes and risky guesses.
  6. Start typing now: 10-15 minutes daily is better than a last-minute crash course after Tier 1.
  7. Keep current affairs rolling: revise monthly notes and use short daily quizzes to prevent backlog.
  8. Use PYQs as a filter: previous papers show how SSC frames familiar concepts and which sub-topics repeat.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Has SSC introduced a sectional timer in CHSL Tier 1 2026?

Yes. The official SSC CHSL 2026 notice gives Tier 1 a total of 60 minutes with a fixed 15-minute timer for each of the four subjects. Eligible scribe candidates get 20 minutes per subject, for a total of 80 minutes.

What is the SSC CHSL Tier 1 syllabus 2026?

Tier 1 covers English Language, General Intelligence, Quantitative Aptitude and General Awareness. Each subject has 25 questions for 50 marks, making 100 questions and 200 marks in total.

Is there negative marking in SSC CHSL 2026?

Yes. Tier 1 deducts 0.50 mark for each wrong answer. Tier 2 deducts 1 mark for each wrong answer in Sections I, II and III.

Does SSC CHSL Tier 2 also have sectional timers?

Yes. In Section I, Mathematical Abilities and Reasoning get 30 minutes each. In Section II, English gets 40 minutes and General Awareness gets 20 minutes. Computer Knowledge has a separate 15-minute slot.

What typing speed is required for SSC CHSL LDC/JSA?

The standard is 35 wpm in English or 30 wpm in Hindi. The regular typing test lasts 10 minutes. Candidates choosing Hindi must use Mangal Font.

Are Tier 1 marks counted in SSC CHSL final merit?

No. Tier 1 is used for shortlisting to Tier 2. Final merit is based on the aggregate marks of Tier 2 Section I and Section II, subject to qualifying Computer Knowledge and the Skill/Typing Test.

Does SSC publish official topic-wise weightage?

SSC does not provide a fixed chapter-wise question allocation in the official syllabus. Topic-weightage tables should be treated as previous-paper trends for revision planning, not guaranteed counts.
